The blur-between-colours principle.
The framing you ended on is structurally important and worth naming as a memo principle in its own right. Not so isolatedly different that it looks like a prop. Not so overlapping that archetypes blur into each other. Each one a prop hitting one note alone is wrong; each one part of a spectrum where the blur between colours is exactly that — a blur, not a line.
This is the difference between a cast and an ecology. A cast is a list of distinct functions. An ecology is a continuous field with dense regions and sparse regions and gradual transitions. The book renders an ecology, not a cast. The clans are dense regions on a continuous field, not labelled boxes in a typology. A man from one clan can drift to another. A man can be in two clans simultaneously by attending two different bars. A clan can dissolve and reform. The boundaries are real but are not lines. They are the kind of boundaries that exist between sand and water on a beach — a zone, varying, observable, never crisp.
This is also a writing-discipline principle. When rendering any of the clans, the prose should not produce a clean type-rendering. The clan is a tendency, not a definition. The men in the clan are not all the same and the differences within are part of the texture. A weekend warrior who has been doing it for fifteen years is becoming a fallen angel. A nepo baby who keeps coming back longer than his peers is on the same trajectory. The book's geography is verb-shaped, not noun-shaped — what each man is doing, not what each man is. The reader assembles the typology from the verbs and the typology is not stated.

On the Fallen Angel as the only other one like Joe.
Push me on this if I am wrong, but I think the answer is yes, with calibration.
The case for: the book benefits from one other figure who does not perform in the bar's standard register. Joe-not-gossiping is one structural fact. If Joe is the only such figure, the not-gossiping reads as exceptional — Joe's particular condition, his depletion, his peculiarity. If there is one other man at the table with a parallel quietness, the not-gossiping becomes a configuration two men in the clan share, which is more interesting because it suggests the depletion has more than one route to it. Joe arrived at it through his arc; the Fallen Angel arrived through his fall; both end up at the same place. Two trajectories converging at the same silence. The reader sees that the silence is not Joe's individual psychology but a structural endpoint that more than one path leads to.
The case against: putting two non-performers at the same table risks under-populating the clan. The bar dynamic the book has been building depends on the constant verbal production of the others — gossip, rounds, claims, jokes, the rumours about Danger Mouse, the conquistador-castration-thesis articulations. If two of the four or five core members are quiet, the verbal weight shifts to the remaining loud ones and the book may need them louder than they should be, which costs realism.
The calibration that probably resolves it: the Fallen Angel is not gossiping in the same depleted register as Joe, but his quiet has different texture. Joe's quiet is depletion-of-energy. The Fallen Angel's quiet is concealment — he was somebody once, the clan knows it vaguely, the specifics are protected, and he does not contribute the standard claims about who he knew, what he did, how much money he made because contributing would force specifics. Joe's quiet has nothing underneath; the Fallen Angel's quiet has something specific underneath that he is keeping intact. Different mechanisms. Same surface effect: he is at the table and he is not contributing to the verbal apparatus.
This means the Fallen Angel is not Joe's mirror. He is a man who has kept something whilst losing everything else, and the keeping is one of the few things he still has. Joe has kept nothing. The two of them at the same table look similar from outside and are different from inside. Worth holding both for the writer's knowledge.
When the banker recognition happens, the Fallen Angel deflates not because his secret is exposed to the clan (the clan already roughly knew) but because the secret is now being told to a fresh audience — the banker's group at the other table — which means his old life is still circulating somewhere outside the bar, still has gossip-velocity, has not been forgotten. He had been in the bar long enough to think the past had quieted. The banker's recognition is the proof that it has not. That is the deflation. The thing he has been keeping is still being told, possibly being told accurately, possibly being told inaccurately, and he does not know which version is being passed across that table now.
This calibration has another payoff: it gives the Fallen Angel a small interior the book can render glancingly, without making him a co-protagonist. He is the man who keeps. He is the man who flinches when the past is still alive somewhere. He is the man Joe recognises, vaguely, as having a different relationship to silence than he himself has. Joe registers this difference at the level of texture. He does not analyse it. The reader sees the difference and supplies the analysis.
In, with the calibration: the Fallen Angel and Joe are both quiet at the table; their quiets are structurally different; the difference is rendered through small physical and behavioural detail, never through dialogue or interior commentary. The two men have a relationship of recognised similarity without ever discussing it. They are seated next to each other at the bar more often than chance would predict. They drink at similar paces. They are sometimes the last two left when the others have gone home with women. They do not become friends in any active register; they become companions in the same quiet, which is something the book can render without naming.

Better. Hybrid solves the under-populating concern and gives the Fallen Angel his own specific mechanism that is neither Joe's silence nor the clan's noise.
He talks. He participates. The clan does not see him as the quiet one. He has stories, he buys his rounds, he holds his end of the conversation. The difference is in the texture of what he says — he plays his past down. The clan's standard verbal register is inflation: I knew, I did, I made, I fucked. The Fallen Angel's register is the inverse: when his past comes up, he deflects, minimises, changes the subject quickly. He does not lie. He just makes everything he was sound smaller than it was. The clan reads this as modesty or as the natural reluctance of a man with pride to discuss what he has lost. They do not read it as concealment, because they have no model for concealment in their own social lives. Why would a man play himself down. Inconceivable. The clan therefore underestimates his actual past consistently and the Fallen Angel actively maintains the underestimation.
This makes him the only man in the clan who is better at concealment than the others are at exposure. He has been doing this for years. He is fluent at it. The clan thinks they know what he was; they know roughly a third of it. He has been keeping the other two-thirds intact for the entire span of his presence at the bar.
The banker recognition is therefore catastrophic in a specific way. The banker is going to tell his table the actual version. Not the down-played version. The version that includes the title, the salary, the role, the company name, the responsibilities. The version with the actual numbers. The banker has no reason to play it down because the banker is not the Fallen Angel. The banker is informing his colleagues about a man they may have heard about in the financial press a decade ago, and the informing will be at the level of truth. The Fallen Angel cannot stop this from happening at the next table. He can only manage what happens at his own.
