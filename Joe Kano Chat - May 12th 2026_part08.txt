If Joe pings it, even partially, the book becomes the story of a man who began to see the system from inside it. That is a recognisable literary arc — late-life dim recognition, the protagonist starting to perceive what has been around him. Greene runs versions of this. Coetzee's Lurie runs a version of this. The arc has dignity, even quiet dignity, because the protagonist's interior is doing work across the book that culminates in some small late awareness. The reader has been promised this arc by literary convention and is gratified to receive it.
If Joe does not ping it, the book becomes the story of a man who lived inside a system without ever seeing it. That is a harder arc and a less common one. The book renders the system; the reader sees the system; the protagonist remains inside without ever rising to the perceptual position the reader occupies. The protagonist's interior continues to operate at depleted register throughout, never producing the late awareness that would otherwise be the book's earned reward. The reader leaves the book with the system visible in a way no character in the book ever achieved.
These are different propositions about what the book is and what reading it is for.
The first proposition: literature as the record of a man's coming-to-awareness, however dim. The protagonist is the privileged consciousness through whom the world is partly understood. The reader's understanding emerges alongside the protagonist's, with the reader running slightly ahead and the protagonist arriving at the recognitions in his own time. This is the standard novelistic contract from Henry James onward, more or less.
The second proposition: literature as the record of a perceptual gap between the reader and the protagonist, where the protagonist is the occasion through which the world is rendered but is not himself the consciousness that perceives it. The reader perceives. The protagonist lives. The two operations run in parallel and never converge. The book's empathy mechanism depends on the gap rather than on the protagonist's eventual closure of it.
The book has been built toward the second proposition all along. Every architectural decision so far has reinforced it. The harbinger track that Joe never recognises. The kano-moment that produces drift rather than awareness. The mother's funeral that he attends without articulating what the room reveals. Mother Mary's snap that he registers without parsing. The Marx-talk that he absorbs without seeing through. The not-gossiping principle, the observation-versus-judgement distinction, the dense-but-uncomprehending interior — all of these are technical implementations of the protagonist who lives inside without rising to perception.
If Joe pings the construction-economy at any meaningful level, even partially, even involuntarily, the book quietly shifts into the first proposition. The whole architecture starts working toward the eventual ping. The reader begins reading for the moment Joe almost-understands. The book's centre of gravity moves from the rendering of the system to the protagonist's growth toward the system's perception. Once the centre moves, every prior architectural decision starts being read in the new register. The harbingers become foreshadowing-of-eventual-recognition. The kano-moment becomes preparation-for-late-awareness. The not-gossiping becomes proto-wisdom. The book becomes the kind of book it has been refusing to be.
This is a structural consequence, not a moral one. The ping-version is a perfectly defensible book. It is the more common book of its kind. It would be easier to write. Most contemporary literary fiction operates inside this proposition. The reader has been trained on it.
The no-ping version is the harder book and the one the architecture has been preparing. It has fewer precedents in pure form. Coetzee in Disgrace allows Lurie one small pinging late — the dog, the sentence about the dog, the reader's recognition that Lurie has accessed something. Even Coetzee did not fully resist the ping. McCarthy in No Country allows Bell the dream-monologue at the end. The pure no-ping book is rarer. Stoner has Stoner achieve something close to recognition near death; The Stranger gives Meursault his philosophical clarity at the end. Most books, even severe ones, give the protagonist a final ping.
Books that withhold the ping entirely are unusual and the reader sometimes responds to them as if something has been denied them. The reader has expected the protagonist's awareness as part of the contract. Withholding it can read as cruelty or as failure. The book has to earn the withholding by making the gap between reader and protagonist itself the content. The reader has to feel that the gap is the book's argument, not an oversight.
The argument the no-ping book makes is that men like Joe do not, in fact, achieve the perceptual position the literary convention promises them. The book is honest about what depletion at this scale actually produces. It does not produce wisdom. It does not produce late awareness. It produces continuation. The protagonist proceeds to whatever ending the book gives him without rising. The reader leaves having seen what the protagonist could not see. The reader is left with the responsibility of having seen it. The book gives the reader the system and refuses to give the reader the consolation of the protagonist also seeing it.
This is a particular literary-moral position. It is not for every book. It is consistent with what this book has been building.
The variant you raised — flawed or incomplete pinging — splits the difference and probably belongs to the first proposition rather than the second. Even a flawed ping is a ping. Once the protagonist is granted partial awareness, the reader reads the partiality as on a continuum with awareness rather than as categorically different. He almost understood is structurally similar to he understood for purposes of the book's arc; the reader receives both as the protagonist arriving, however incompletely, at the perceptual position. The flawed-ping is a softer version of the ping; it is not a third option.
If you want the flawed-ping, the book is in the first proposition. If you want no ping, the book is in the second. There is not a stable middle position.
