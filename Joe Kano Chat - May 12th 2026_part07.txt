The blur-between-colours principle.
The framing you ended on is structurally important and worth naming as a memo principle in its own right. Not so isolatedly different that it looks like a prop. Not so overlapping that archetypes blur into each other. Each one a prop hitting one note alone is wrong; each one part of a spectrum where the blur between colours is exactly that — a blur, not a line.
This is the difference between a cast and an ecology. A cast is a list of distinct functions. An ecology is a continuous field with dense regions and sparse regions and gradual transitions. The book renders an ecology, not a cast. The clans are dense regions on a continuous field, not labelled boxes in a typology. A man from one clan can drift to another. A man can be in two clans simultaneously by attending two different bars. A clan can dissolve and reform. The boundaries are real but are not lines. They are the kind of boundaries that exist between sand and water on a beach — a zone, varying, observable, never crisp.
This is also a writing-discipline principle. When rendering any of the clans, the prose should not produce a clean type-rendering. The clan is a tendency, not a definition. The men in the clan are not all the same and the differences within are part of the texture. A weekend warrior who has been doing it for fifteen years is becoming a fallen angel. A nepo baby who keeps coming back longer than his peers is on the same trajectory. The book's geography is verb-shaped, not noun-shaped — what each man is doing, not what each man is. The reader assembles the typology from the verbs and the typology is not stated.
