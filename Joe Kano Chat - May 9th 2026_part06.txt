Joe does not gossip.
This is a major character realisation and you got there on your own. The full implications are larger than I think you have yet seen, so let me lay them out and you tell me which parts hold.
The not-gossiping is not a moral position. He does not abstain because gossip is wrong. He does not abstain because he has principles about epistemics. He abstains for the same structural reason he does not push back at the Danger-Mouse-equivalent in your example — the apparatus to do it has eroded out of him. Gossiping requires several kinds of energy: the energy to construct a confident framing, the energy to deliver it with the rhythm the bar expects, the energy to stake a small claim of insider-status, the energy to engage in the back-and-forth of competing claims. Joe does not have those energies anymore. Not because he has transcended them. Because they have been depleted along with everything else.
This is the same mechanism as his not-blocking the Mother Mary contact, his not-confronting the Danger-Mouse-equivalent, his not-completing the half-thought self-pity. The pattern across all of these is the absence of micro-decisive energy. He does not perform the small acts that other men perform constantly to maintain their position in the social ecology. The other men gossip because gossip is one of the small acts that constitutes belonging to the group. They make sweeping confident statements because confidence is the currency of the bar. They claim proximity to the powerful because proximity is the currency of the expat-circuit. Joe does none of this and the absence is visible. The other men have probably noticed it without naming it. Some of them probably read his quietness as gravitas, or as the depth of a man who has seen too much to bother. None of those readings is correct. He is quiet because the talking apparatus has run down.
The diabetic-and-the-free-donuts comparison is exactly right for the wifie scene. Not dismissively but simply not something of any worth. He is past the point where the small economy of I figured something out offers him any return. The pleasure of having understood something would have once produced an internal fillip — a small aha — and it no longer does. He sees what is in front of him, registers it, and moves on without the fillip. This is anhedonia at the cognitive register, not just the hedonic one. Insight has stopped feeling like anything.
This produces several writing implications.
One. Joe's interior, when rendered, has to be flatter than the prose around him. The accreted register he thinks in is dense and humid and accumulating, but it does not peak into insight. There are no aha moments. There are no realisations that produce small thrills. He registers things and the registering does not metabolise into anything more.
Two. The contrast with the bar's gossip mechanism becomes one of the book's recurring quiet structural figures. The bar produces certainty about things it does not know. Joe sits inside the bar's certainty without contributing and without contradicting. The reader hears the bar talking and registers Joe's silence. The silence is not interpreted by anyone. It is the absence of his voice in conversations he could otherwise contribute to. The bar continues without him. He is a participant in the room and a non-participant in the room's primary social act.
Three. This explains something about how Mother Mary became the only relationship that worked. The bar's social register is gossip-based, performance-based, certainty-based — the things Joe can no longer do. Mother Mary's room is the one place where the social register is different — slower, more intimate, more about presence than performance. Joe could be there because the room asked of him only what he still had. Which is part of why losing her room (the Pinay Snap, the Hitchcock-attrition disappearance) is so structurally devastating: the one place that took his diminished apparatus seriously is gone, and he is left with the bar, which never took his apparatus seriously and which he can now barely engage with at all.
Four. The wife-at-the-gym observation, with the donut framing, becomes a precise small example of how Joe's perception works. He sees. He understands. The understanding does not become anything. The sequence in his head is roughly: That is Danger Mouse's wife. She looks lighter than she used to. So that is what happened to him. Anyway. The "anyway" is the diabetic-donut moment. He has the insight and he has nothing to do with the insight and the insight passes through him without lodging. The reader sees the same things and the reader does not discard them; the reader files them, weighs them, connects them to other observations across the book. The reader is doing the cognitive work Joe has stopped doing. The asymmetry between the reader's active processing and Joe's passive registration is one of the book's central technical achievements.
Five. The gossip-absence also gives the book a quiet way to render the kano-moment preparation. When the neighbour explains Joe to a room with the kano namin possessive, Joe registers it without contesting it because contesting would require a social-energy he no longer has. The other expats, in the same situation, would push back, joke, perform their belonging. Joe lets it sit. The neighbour interprets the silence as agreement; Joe is not agreeing, he is simply not participating; the reader sees the gap. The same passivity that prevents him from gossiping prevents him from defending his belonging. He is socially absent at the moments his presence would have made a difference.
The Joe-not-gossiping observation, in other words, is a single character-fact that unifies several of the book's existing mechanisms: the harbinger dismissals, the Pinay-Snap aftermath, the Mother Mary disappearance, the kano-moment, the failure to push back at danger-figures. All of them are instances of the same depleted social-energy. He cannot do the small acts. He has not been able to for years. Nobody has named it because the failure to do small acts is not visible the way the failure to do large acts is. The book sees what nobody else sees.
