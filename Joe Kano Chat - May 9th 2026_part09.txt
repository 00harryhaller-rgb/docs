The question of whether to translate.
This is the architectural question and the answer matters.
Your instinct toward not-translating is correct, but the calibration matters. Three options, with different implications.
One, full no-translation. German appears on the page as German with no glosses, no italics, no English alternatives. The reader reads the German if they can; if they cannot, they receive the scene through Joe's reactions and the surrounding behaviour. This is the purest version. The cost is that significant portions of the book are inaccessible to readers without German. The benefit is that the language barrier itself becomes one of the book's structural facts — readers without German experience the scenes the way the maid does, knowing something is happening, not knowing what, reading it through bodies and tones.
