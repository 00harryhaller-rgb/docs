Timeline.
The four-seasons jump version is wrong for this book and you have already half-diagnosed why. The chapter-titled-jumps mechanism imports a structural device that announces here is how the book is organised, and the announcement does work the prose should be doing. It is also the device most readers associate with novels-that-want-to-feel-significant. Cutting to Hot Season as a chapter heading is the literary equivalent of the lead-in-from-Tucker monologue — it tells the reader I am a serious book, observe my structure. The book has been refusing this register throughout.
The Stranger model — two parts, one major break — is the closer correct shape but Camus's break was for a specific reason (the murder, the trial). The book does not have an event of comparable architectural weight that would justify a hard two-part break. The mother's death is structurally important but is not a register-changing event the way the murder is in The Stranger. Joe's life after the mother is recognisably continuous with his life before. The right answer is seamless flow, time passing without announcement. Chapters end. Chapters begin. The walking works.
Several reasons it works structurally where the studio and the workplace-adjacency did not.
One — no competing world. The studio would have been a venue with its own ecology, its own characters, its own atmosphere. The walking has none of those. It is Joe in motion through the city. The city is already in the book. The walking renders the city differently than the bar-and-apartment chapters do, but it does not introduce new ecology. The book's geography expands without acquiring new loci that have to be developed.
Two — no new relationships required. The studio would have demanded a host figure. The workplace-adjacency would have demanded a former colleague. The walking demands no one. Joe walks alone. He passes faces. He stops at places. He moves on. The walks generate texture without generating new architecture. The civilian-register substrate we already locked — the corner store, the buena-mano fruit-seller, the maids in the park, the various small functional encounters — is exactly the substrate the walking renders. The walking is the verb that the civilian-register is the noun-list of. They are the same architecture seen from two angles.
Three — the journey is the destination matches Joe's interior architecture. He has no destination. His apparatus does not access destinations. He is in late-stage drift. Walking is the physical analogue of the drift. The interior depleted-but-present register matches the exterior wandering-but-present register. The two registers reinforce each other instead of competing. A man at a destination has to do something at the destination; a man walking has only to walk. The walking permits the depleted apparatus to operate at its baseline.
Four — the hot pollution catcalls reality is itself useable. You stopped walking in Manila because of the heat, the pollution, the catcalls, and your wife's fury. Joe's walking happens against the same friction. The catcalls — hey Joe, hey kano, where you going — are not romantic urban-flaneur texture. They are part of the kano-mechanism the book has been building. Every walk is a low-grade reactivation of the foreigner-ness Joe has been pretending to forget about. The walk is not pleasant. The walk is something he does because he has nowhere to be and the apartment is the apartment. The friction of the walk is part of why the walk does not produce significance.
Five — the wife forbidding it from before is structurally available. Joe's marriage was estranged or ended some years ago. During the marriage, the wife was furious about the walking — the conquistador-class wife horrified by Joe walking through neighbourhoods she would not enter in her car. The walks are partly a post-wife freedom in the same register that everything else in his life is post-something. The freedom is bare. He goes where he wants. He has no one to report to. The freedom is also empty because there is nothing the freedom is for.

The motion-versus-stasis question.
You raised the question of whether walking introduces a different feel from the rest of the book because it has motion, where most other scenes are stationary (bar, apartment, Marivic's room).
Yes, and the difference is the structural payoff. The walking chapters are physically different from the bar-and-apartment chapters. The prose moves. Joe moves. Things happen alongside him as he moves. He is not anchored to a chair or a bed. The cool register can render walking with its parataxis-precision; the accreted register can render it with its drift. Both registers benefit from the moving-substrate. The book gains a register-variation it would not have without the walking.
The Coetzee comparison is exact here. Disgrace has Lurie in motion repeatedly — the drives between Cape Town and the farm, the walks around the farm, the walks in town. The motion-chapters are some of the book's strongest. They permit Lurie's depleted interior to be rendered against a moving exterior, and the contrast between his stasis and the world's continuance is part of the book's emotional engine. Disgrace without the motion-scenes would be a worse book.
Joe Cano's walking would do the same work. The bar and apartment chapters are dense with stasis. The walking chapters provide the moving register against which the stasis is felt. Without the walking, the book risks being uniformly stationary in a way that flattens its emotional dynamics.
The Power-and-the-Glory dog mechanism.
Your example of the dog he thought would be dead by now and there it is again is precisely the right register for the walking. Not Greene's level of explicit philosophical commentary on hope. There it is again. Period. Joe registers persistence in the city's small lives — the dog still alive, the buena-mano-fruit-seller's child taller than last month, the broken pavement that has been broken for two years and will be broken next year, the new sign that has gone up where the old one was. These are the textures the walking renders. None of them are commented on. They accumulate. The reader feels Joe's relationship to the city through the accumulation.
The discipline against Greene's level of articulation is consistent with the no-ping rule. Joe does not articulate humans are the only animals that can lose hope. He registers that the dog is still there. The reader does the work Greene's priest did. Joe stays at the level of there it is again.
The contemplative-question.
Your concern about the walking permitting too much interiority is real and worth addressing. The risk: a man walking alone for hours has nothing to do but think, and a book that follows him on those walks can drift into the contemplative novel register the architecture has refused.
The discipline: Joe's walking interior is not contemplative. It is the same depleted-but-present register that operates everywhere else in the book. He notices things. He does not develop them. He passes a vendor. He thinks of the price of something. He thinks of his mother's appointment. He thinks of nothing for a stretch. The walk is rendered through the same effects-only rule that governs everything else. The fact that he is alone with his thoughts does not give the book permission to extract a richer interior than the apparatus would actually produce.
The Stranger's Sunday-afternoon-on-the-balcony chapter is the closest reference. Meursault watches people go by. He notices the changing light. He observes without interpreting. Camus does not use Meursault's solitude as an excuse for philosophical interiority. The chapter is rendered at the level of what Meursault registered without the prose elevating the register because no one else is in the room.
Joe's walks operate the same way. He walks. He registers. He moves on. The interior during walking is the same as the interior at the bar or in the apartment — the same depleted apparatus, registering the same texture-of-things-around-him, producing the same not-much-thought.
This means the walking does not become the contemplative chapters or the introspective sections. They are continuous with the rest of the book in register. They differ only in the moving-vs-stationary axis. The interior remains constant.
